Ottawa Climate Damage

How Ottawa Winters Damage Concrete Surfaces

Ottawa winters can be extremely hard on concrete surfaces. Road salt, moisture, freeze-thaw cycles, and temperature fluctuations gradually break down unprotected concrete over time.

  • Road Salt Damage

    Road salt and de-icing chemicals can penetrate concrete pores and gradually weaken the surface, especially in garages and driveways.

  • Freeze-Thaw Cracking

    Water trapped inside concrete expands when frozen, leading to cracks, surface deterioration, and long-term structural wear.

  • Moisture Intrusion

    Basements, garages, and outdoor concrete surfaces can absorb moisture over time, increasing the risk of peeling coatings and surface breakdown.

  • Garage Floor Wear

    Winter vehicle traffic brings snow, slush, salt, and debris into garages, causing abrasion and premature surface deterioration.

  • Peeling & Flaking Coatings

    Low-quality coatings or poorly prepared surfaces often fail under Ottawa's seasonal temperature changes.

  • Surface Dusting & Erosion

    Older concrete surfaces may begin to powder, chip, or wear down due to years of exposure and moisture penetration.

Pro Tip: Concrete coatings last significantly longer when proper grinding, moisture testing, and surface preparation are completed before application.

Prep Matters

Proper Surface Preparation is Key

The durability of any concrete painting project depends on preparation. Poor prep is one of the main reasons paint, epoxy, or coating systems fail. Proper surface preparation helps create a smoother finish, better adhesion, and longer-lasting results.

  • Deep Cleaning

    We remove dirt, dust, grease, mould, and salt residue using proper cleaning methods before any paint or coating is applied.

  • Moisture Testing

    A moisture test confirms whether the concrete surface is ready. One method is taping a plastic sheet to the concrete for 24 hours to check for dampness.

  • Crack Repair

    We repair cracks and damaged areas using concrete crack filler, then allow repairs to dry properly before coating.

  • Etching or Grinding

    Smooth or power-troweled concrete may need etching or grinding to create the right texture for better paint adhesion.

  • Masonry Primer

    A high-quality masonry bonding primer can improve adhesion on porous or previously painted surfaces.

  • Paint & Coating Application

    We apply the right paint, epoxy, sealer, or coating system designed for concrete — usually with at least two coats when recommended by the manufacturer.

Coating Options

Choosing the Right Concrete Coating

The right coating depends on how the space is used, the condition of the concrete, and exposure to moisture, traffic, and weather.

  • Epoxy Coatings

    Best for garage & high-use floors

    Ideal for garage floors and high-use concrete floors. Epoxy creates a durable, polished, and long-lasting finish.

  • Concrete Paint

    Best for basements & utility spaces

    A cost-effective option for lighter-use areas such as basements, concrete walls, or interior utility spaces.

  • Concrete Sealers

    Best for long-term protection

    A clear, durable concrete sealer can help protect paint from stains, moisture, wear, and surface breakdown.

  • Masonry Paint

    Best for brick & vertical surfaces

    Specialized masonry paint is designed for concrete, brick, and masonry surfaces where ordinary house paint would not perform well.

  • Anti-Slip Finishes

    Best for stairs, patios & walkways

    For stairs, patios, walkways, and slippery surfaces, an anti-slip aggregate can be added to the final coat to improve safety.

Pro Tip

Ordinary house paint is not recommended for concrete. Always use products specifically designed for concrete or masonry.
