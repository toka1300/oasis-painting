Prep Matters

How We Prepare Your Deck Before Staining

The difference between a stained deck that lasts and one that peels too soon often comes down to preparation. Our team reviews wood condition, removes surface buildup, and prepares boards so the stain can bond properly.

  • Deck Inspection

    We review boards, railings, stairs, previous stain, peeling areas, cracks, moisture, and high-traffic floor areas.

  • Deck Cleaning / Stripping

    For decks with previous stain or product buildup, we apply a stripping solution and rinse with a hose or controlled pressure washer to help break down the existing coating before sanding.

  • Drying Time

    After washing, the deck usually dries 24 to 48 hours. Staining is only done once moisture levels are suitable to support proper adhesion and curing.

  • Sanding & Surface Prep

    Sanding smooths rough boards, opens wood pores, and improves stain penetration on bare wood and weathered surfaces.

  • Stain Application

    We apply the selected stain system carefully across boards, railings, stairs, panels, and other surfaces.

Stain Options

Choosing the Right Stain for Your Deck

The wrong stain choice is one of the main reasons decks peel or fail early. The best stain depends on your wood type, deck condition, desired colour, maintenance expectations, and how much natural wood grain you want to show.

  • Transparent / Translucent

    Best for natural wood grain

    Often oil-based, these stains penetrate deep into wood while leaving the most natural grain visible.

  • Semi-Transparent

    Best for colour with visible grain

    Adds colour and protection while allowing some wood grain to show through. Typically lasts around 2 to 3 years depending on exposure.

  • Solid Stains

    Best for more coverage

    Creates a more paint-like appearance with little to no visible grain. Often more durable than paint for walking surfaces and may last around 3 to 5 years.

  • Oil-Based Stains

    Best for deep penetration

    Offers strong moisture protection and can soak deep into wood pores, though some products may have higher VOCs.

Weather & Timing

When Is the Best Time to Stain a Deck in Ottawa?

Deck staining depends heavily on weather. The best results happen when the wood is dry, temperatures are stable, and rain isn't expected. In Ottawa, late spring through early fall is usually the best window.

  • Ideal Temperature

    Best results between 10°C and 30°C with stable temperatures.

  • Low Humidity

    Humidity under 70% and no rain forecast within 12 to 24 hours.

  • Best Window

    Late spring through early fall — the summer months are typically ideal.

  • Pre-Winter Sealer

    A fresh coat of sealer in early fall helps wood withstand snow and freeze-thaw cycles.

New Pressure Treated Wood?

New pressure treated wood usually needs time to dry before staining. In many cases, homeowners should wait around 2 to 6 months, depending on the wood, exposure, and moisture level.

Deck Care Tips

How to Maintain a Stained Deck

A stained deck needs regular care to stay protected. Simple maintenance can help prevent moisture damage, peeling, mildew, and premature fading.

  • Use the Water Test

    Place a small amount of water on the surface. If it beads up, the sealant is still protecting the wood. If it soaks in, it may be time to reseal or restain.

  • Keep the Surface Clean

    Remove leaves, dirt, mold, mildew, and algae before they sit too long. Organic buildup traps moisture and wears down the finish.

  • Use a Plastic Shovel in Winter

    Use plastic shovels rather than metal for snow removal to help prevent scratching or damaging the stain.

  • Watch for Peeling or Gray Wood

    If your deck starts peeling, cracking, fading, or turning gray, it may need cleaning, sanding, or a new stain system.

  • Plan Seasonal Maintenance

    A fresh coat of sealer in early fall can help your deck withstand Ottawa's winter freeze-thaw cycles and heavy snow.
